What is Imagery?
Definition + five senses.
🎤 What to remember
Imagery is descriptive language that helps readers imagine what they see, hear, smell, taste, or feel. The five senses are sight, sound, smell, taste, and touch.
Slide 3 of 11
Imagery Example – Ratatouille
Why does the image prove imagery?
🎤 What to remember
Ratatouille is a good imagery example because its food scenes use vivid colors and textures that make us imagine what the food would be like.
Slide 4 of 11
How Ratatouille Uses Imagery
Connect it to the senses.
🎤 What to remember
We see colorful food, hear sizzling and cooking, imagine the aroma and flavor, and imagine the texture and warmth. It makes the food feel like a sensory experience.
Slide 5 of 11
Why Writers Use Imagery
Explain the purpose.
🎤 What to remember
Writers use imagery to create mood, make scenes vivid, build emotion, help readers imagine what is happening, and make writing more memorable.

What is Juxtaposition?
Define + contrast.
🎤 What to remember
Juxtaposition is putting two things close together so their differences or similarities stand out. Light next to dark makes the contrast easier to notice.
Slide 8 of 11
Cinderella
What is contrasted?
🎤 What to remember
Cinderella and her stepsisters are juxtaposed because Cinderella is kind and humble while the stepsisters are cruel and selfish. Their differences stand out more when shown against each other.

Teacher questions
Fast answers
What is imagery?
Sensory/descriptive details that help the audience imagine an experience.
Need all five senses?
No. One or several can create imagery.
Why Ratatouille?
Color, sound, texture, aroma and flavor make the food vivid.
What is juxtaposition?
Putting two things together so their differences or similarities stand out.
Why Cinderella?
Cinderella's kind/humble traits contrast with the stepsisters' cruel/selfish traits.
